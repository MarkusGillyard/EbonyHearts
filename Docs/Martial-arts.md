# Keep the frames, move the bodies

Mortal Slopbat's martial arts will look and feel more real mainly by changing what the bodies do *inside* the existing frame data, not by changing the frame data. Real techniques do not fit game timing: a whole-body jab takes about **405 ms (24 frames)** and a Muay Thai roundhouse about **1.02 s** from lift-off to landing ([Stanley et al. 2018](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/rpanxx/v18y2018i5p835-854.html); [Gavagan & Sayers 2017](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0182645)), so every leading fighting game compresses startup, adds tells in the follow-through and exaggerates contact, as Slopbat's 5-frame jab and 13-frame roundhouse already do. The most valuable work is presentation-only, in four parts: re-key every strike on the measured kinetic chain, so the feet and hips move first and the fist or foot is still travelling fast when it lands (today a cubic ease-out stops it dead on the first active frame and then holds it); give each fighter the stance, guard, rhythm and footwork their style is known by; reshape the hit-stop freeze along Sakurai's published recipe; and choose hit reactions by the geometry of the strike, so a hook twists the head and an uppercut lifts the chin. Next come quieter camera shake, impact sound split by face, body, leg and weapon, combos that flow from one pose into the next, and believable knockdowns and get-ups. Only a few simulation changes justify their cost: counter-hit hit-stop applied to the victim alone, a push kick (teep) and a shin check for Ashka, period throws for Bram, and push-outs for Gorm. If licensed motion capture is added, it should supply idles, walks, falls, get-ups and pose reference rather than replace the key-pose strikes, and physics should start only once a knockout is decided, because a deterministic 60 Hz simulation needs authored outcomes. The research is strong on strike mechanics and hit-stop practice, thin on hit-reaction systems and measured guard geometry, and silent on the winged boss.

## Ten presentation changes outrank every rule change

Slopbat's architecture makes this order cheap to follow. The simulation in `Combat/` is plain deterministic C++ that emits events. The animator in `Anim/` turns each integer-frame state into key poses, eases, IK and springs (`Docs/Architecture.md`). Hitboxes and hurtboxes are authored per frame as boxes relative to the fighter's root (`Combat/SlopData.h`), not read from bones. So anything that changes only poses, curves, springs, camera, effects or sound leaves frame data, Trials and the AI untouched, and it cannot change who hits whom.

The ranking below weighs how much each change improves realism, accuracy, look and feel against what it costs. Effort is a rough figure for one developer: **S** means up to two days, **M** one to three weeks, **L** a month or more.

### Slopbat's strikes stop dead on the target, the opposite of real ones

The shared basics key each strike as load, then hit, then hold, then recover (`Content/Data/basics.json`). The jab goes from `hp_load` at frame 2 to `hp_hit` at frame 5 with the `snap` ease, holds `hp_hit` unchanged to frame 8, and returns to stance by frame 17. `snap` is 1 − (1 − t)³ (`Anim/SlopPose.cpp`), a cubic ease-out. Across the jab's three extension frames the hand covers about **70%, 96% and 100%** of the distance. It arrives with zero velocity, then stays frozen through the active frames. Before the follow-through springs act on it, that is the motion profile of a pulled punch: the biggest jump comes straight out of the load, the hand stops on the target, and the pose freezes. Tekken's animation director says exactly this kind of freeze on the hit frame "looks cheap" ([Denfaminicogamer](https://news.denfaminicogamer.jp/kikakuthetower/241227u)). The steelman for the current curve is that it reads as snappy and responsive, which is why front-loaded eases are common. But the measured data runs the other way. A fist peaks shortly before contact and still has about 78% of its peak speed when it lands ([PMC9798280](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9798280/)). A kicking foot slows to about half its peak by impact, after the knee's extension has already peaked ([Gavagan & Sayers 2017](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0182645)).

The fix is a new `strike` ease and one extra key per move. The ease's speed should peak at roughly 70–85% of the segment and still be at half to four-fifths of that peak on arrival (feet nearer half, fists nearer four-fifths). For a three-frame jab extension, a profile like 30% / 70% / 100% fits. The extra key is a "drive-through" pose a few centimetres past `hp_hit` across the active frames, followed by retraction along the same line. These targets are design proposals derived from the measurements, not published game values. Inside the load key, the chain should be visible in the body, not only the arm. On the jab the lead foot pushes and the hips and shoulders turn slightly. On the cross the rear heel turns out and the hips rotate further than for the jab, which the lead-straight study found uses less trunk rotation than the rear punch ([PMC9798280](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9798280/)). On the roundhouse the support heel turns out and rises onto the ball of the foot ([Fairtex](https://fairtexstore.com/en-nl/blogs/news/how-to-throw-a-thai-roundhouse-kick-breaking-down-the-technique)). Throughout, the non-striking hand stays at the chin.

Ishiguro's CEDEC 2026 talk supplies the matching rules of thumb. Over-lunged "poster" poses, with the knee pushed past the ankle and the head leaning in, drop weight straight down and look weak. Readability beats speed. Individuality belongs in the follow-through, not the impact pose ([4Gamer](https://www.4gamer.net/games/991/G999104/20260728025/)). Because the boxes are authored separately, moving the arm a few centimetres cannot change a hit. The only constraint is the one Eric Jacobus describes from his mocap work: the active pose must "hit this certain pose, cross this certain hit-box" ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/features/interviews/interview-the-youtube-star-who-became-kratos/)).

### The freeze needs to decay, drift and land on the target

Hit-stop is per fighter in the simulation. A clean hit freezes attacker and victim for the same number of frames: 5, 8 or 11 by damage, +2 for blows that send a body flying, +2 on a counter hit, and 2 fewer on block (`Combat/SlopFightSim.cpp`). During the freeze the animator moves the victim sideways as sin(95t), at an amplitude of up to 2.4 cm that does not change over the freeze. It never shakes vertically in the air, and nothing blends into the hurt pose (`FAnimator::Impact` and `Update` in `Anim/SlopAnimator.cpp`).

Sakurai's column and video give the full recipe. Shake the victim more than the attacker. Shake horizontally on the ground and vertically in the air, so feet do not clip into the floor. Let the shake decay over the freeze, computed from its total length. Keep hurtboxes still. Blend from the flinch into the hurt pose over about four frames. Let the attacker creep forward imperceptibly. Scale the shake with camera distance ([Source Gaming](https://sourcegaming.info/2015/11/11/thoughts-on-hitstop-sakurais-famitsu-column-vol-490-1/); [Nintendo Wire](https://nintendowire.com/news/2022/12/12/this-week-in-sakurai-12-5-12-11-fine-tuning-hit-stop-and-cheating-the-system/)). Smash also keeps particle effects animating through the freeze, which stops it reading as a frame drop ([SmashWiki](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Hitlag)).

Slopbat can add one technique the sources do not document for SF, Tekken or MK, which is pinning contact. While both fighters are frozen, the existing hand and foot IK can pull the striking fist or shin onto the event's contact point and hold it there for the freeze. That gives the "lingering impression with a small motion at impact" Tekken aims for, and nothing in the simulation reads it. The drive-through key from item 1 supplies the attacker's small motion.

### Hit reactions should know a hook from a straight

The victim today gets one of four reaction poses chosen by stun type (`hit_head`, `hit_body`, `hit_gut`, `hit_low`). On top of that, springs push the head, chest and hips straight back, weighted by the height of the blow (`Anim/SlopAnimator.cpp`). Every punch to the face therefore snaps the head the same way.

The fix is a presentation-only impact tag on each move. It would give a direction in the fighting plane, a twist sign and size, and a target (jaw, chest, liver or thigh), and it would drive new yaw and side-bend springs and pick from reaction variants. The mapping follows impact geometry and the measured head loads. A straight drives the head back and lifts the chin. A hook turns the head away and bends it sideways while the body lags, which is the largest rotational case and the classic knockout. An uppercut extends the neck and rocks the weight onto the heels. A head kick produces the largest whole-head displacement. These directions are inferences: the accelerations come from crash-test dummies and instrumented mouthguards, not from video of real heads ([Walilko 2005](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/39/10/710); [Boroushak 2021](https://www.mjssm.me/clanci/MJSSM_March_2021_Boroushak_31-35.pdf)). Body shots should fold the torso toward the impact and drop the elbows to cover. Leg kicks should buckle the struck knee and shift the weight onto the other leg. Both are coaching consensus rather than measurement.

Two or three variants per category, chosen deterministically, will stop repetition. Tekken 8 simulated multiple destruction variants so the same movement never repeats ([Autodesk](https://blogs.autodesk.com/media-and-entertainment/2025/01/28/tekken-8-legacy-meets-innovation-with-the-combined-power-of-maya-and-unreal-engine/)). The hitstun length stays exactly as authored.

### Lower-ranked items are cheap, but each needs restraint

Camera shake now fires on every hit and grows with strength, with extra shake on heavy hits and counters, plus a kick along the blow on heavy ones (`View/SlopMatch.cpp`). God of War's designers accepted losing some sense of impact in exchange for legibility ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=370399)). The better split for Slopbat is no shake on light and medium hits, the directional kick on heavy hits, and real shake only for knockdowns, KOs and stage events.

Likewise, every clean hit now lights the victim's body. Keeping that flash for counters and heavy hits, and using a brief spray of sweat on head hits and a puff of dust on body hits and landings, is a better match for "stylised realism". That proposal is a synthesis, not something a source states. Fighting-game players already read spark size as damage and hit-effect type as hit type ([VF forum](https://host.virtuafighter.com/threads/hit-flashes-sparks-effects-auras-whats-the-difference.21696)). Fight Night Champion's stun cue, a slight camera shift plus a faint whistle ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fight_Night_Champion)), suits Slopbat's dizzy state.

Sound is where realism costs least. Slopbat synthesises a low thump, a mid slap and a high crack, and layers recorded CC0 punches under them (`Docs/Design.md`). Mortal Kombat 1 gives body hits more "crunch", face hits more "squish" and blade hits a "sharp and painful" edge. It shares core punch and kick impacts across characters but customises weapon sounds per character ([A Sound Effect](https://www.asoundeffect.com/mortal-kombat-1-game-audio/)). That maps onto Slopbat as separate families for face, body, leg, block and shin-on-shin check, plus per-character weapon sets: wood for Bram's truncheon, brass and cable for Hollis, chain rattle and blade for Nix. MK1 records over 50 effort categories and 300–400 vocal files per character. Bourne found that too few effort variants made overused attacks stand out. It also found that sounds placed on animation notifies misfired under slow motion and frame hitches ([Designing Sound](https://designingsound.org/2011/04/22/rodney-gates-special-creating-hand-to-hand-combat-audio-for-robert-ludlums-the-bourne-conspiracy/)). Slopbat already triggers sound from simulation events, which is the right design, and should keep doing so.

For chains, the end pose of each dial-a-combo link should be the start pose of the next, which is Absolver's model. Cancels should record the pose offset and let it decay (inertialization) rather than easing from wherever the body happens to be ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025165/Inertialization-High-Performance-Animation-Transitions)).

Throws should begin with visible kuzushi (off-balancing), such as a snap pull that tips the victim onto the toes. Judo studies find that the person being thrown resists during this phase, and that their centre of mass drops while their rotation builds during the throw ([Imamura et al. 2006](https://jssm.org/jssm-05-CSSI1-122.xml-abst); [seoi-nage uke study](https://lida.sport-iat.de/dbv/Record/4092983?lng=en)).

Knockouts should separate a flash knockdown, where the fighter posts a hand and recovers, from a clean knockout, where the body falls as a unit with no protective arm. In about two-thirds of the knockouts studied on video, a "fencing response" holds one forearm rigidly in the air for up to several seconds ([ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2009/09/090902133643.htm)). The flash-versus-clean distinction itself is clinical common knowledge, not measured fall data.

Get-ups for the human fighters should use the technical stand-up. The fighter posts a hand behind the hip, plants the same-side foot, keeps the other hand up as a frame, lifts the hips and sweeps the extended leg back ([Grapplearts](https://www.grapplearts.com/the-four-variations-of-the-bjj-technical-standup/)).

For secondary motion, Slopbat's follow-through springs should extend to the coat tails, veil, ponytail, hood, apron and chain. This would also address the known art problem that "Mirela's skirt lifts flat in high kicks" (`Docs/Handoff.md`).

Changes to the simulation affect balance, the AI and the Trials tests, so they belong after the presentation work and each needs a playtest. Hit-stop is per fighter in the simulation (separate `A.Hitstop` and `D.Hitstop`), so the first item is structurally easy.

The counter-hit change is the cheapest real improvement to feel. Today a counter freezes both fighters 2 frames longer and stuns the victim 4 frames longer (`StunBonus` in `SlopFightSim.cpp`). Under the GBVSR model, the victim alone would freeze an extra 2–8 frames by strength and the stun bonus would shrink by the same amount. The counter would then read as a visibly bigger blow while frame advantage stays exactly as Trials and the AI expect, and the victim's longer freeze doubles as a clear "you countered" signal.

Ashka's teep should be a command normal: lead-leg for speed and range-finding, rear-leg for stopping an advance ([Evolve MMA](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/mastering-the-teep-how-to-use-muay-thais-push-kick-to-control-distance/)). It should do little damage and push a long way. Her check would work like Slopbat's existing parry, which today accepts only high, mid and overhead attacks, as a short move that catches low kicks with the shin.

For Gorm, the cheapest version of "sumo" in the rules is more pushback on his thrusting strikes, so pushing the opponent toward the edge becomes his game. For Bram, throws already carry a victim path in data (`FThrowDef::Path`). A chancery (headlock-and-punch) or a cross-buttock (hip throw) can keep the existing total frames, damage frame and release point, which keeps balance neutral. It needs new multi-hit logic only if each punch of the chancery is to deal its own damage.

The leg-damage option should ship off by default, if it ships at all. Even EA keeps its most dramatic damage event rare: UFC 5's doctor stoppages are "relatively rare, so they won't feel intrusive" ([EA](https://www.ea.com/ar/games/ufc/ufc-5/news/ufc-5-gameplay)). A limp drawn only in the idle and walk animations, with walk speed unchanged, gives most of the realism with no balance cost.

## Real timing does not fit a button press, so realism moves into the chain and the follow-through

Realism and game feel collide most sharply over timing. Measured whole-body punches take about 405 ms for a jab and roughly 650 ms for a lead hook (derived from the study's reported percentages). A roundhouse goes from onset to contact in about **0.28–0.37 s (17–22 frames)**, and a Muay Thai roundhouse spends **650 ms (39 frames)** returning to the floor, about two-thirds of the whole kick ([Stanley et al. 2018](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/rpanxx/v18y2018i5p835-854.html); [PMC10459763](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10459763/); [Gavagan & Sayers 2017](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0182645)). Slopbat's jab starts in 5 frames, its high kick in 9 and its roundhouse in 13, with 22 frames of recovery (`Content/Data/basics.json`). Those numbers already compress reality hard, and they should stay that way. The table sets out where the two pull apart and how the sources resolve each conflict.

| Where they pull apart | What real fighters do | What the game needs | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strike timing | A whole-body jab takes ~405 ms, and a defender reacting from cold needs 264–299 ms just to start moving ([Falco et al. 2011](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/entities/publication/6ec51cae-96ad-40db-96ff-e54e5c328888)), so real defence relies on anticipation and guard position | A press must show motion within ~6 frames; startups of 5–13 frames | Keep the frames. Show the arm or extension phase plus a compressed chain. Tekken drops the stance frame so a press reacts at once ([Denfaminicogamer](https://news.denfaminicogamer.jp/kikakuthetower/241227u)) |
| Telegraphing | A kickboxing switch straight comes with no windup | Moves must be readable and punishable | Tekken adds "deliberate tells in the follow-through" to Bryan's Cannon Straight ([Denfaminicogamer](https://news.denfaminicogamer.jp/kikakuthetower/241227u)) |
| Recovery | The recoil takes about 69% of a roundhouse | 22 recovery frames | Compress the recoil but keep its path: the shin returns, the foot lands, the guard comes back |
| Contact | Nothing freezes in a real fight | Weight and a confirmed hit | Hit-stop. God of War holds the first hit frame, "not realistic" but it sells the connection ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=370399)). Linger on the pose rather than freezing dead |
| Defence against low kicks | A shin check | MK3's crouch-block rule | Keep the rule, give Ashka a Thai-style low guard pose, and add the check as an extra move |
| Jumps | Real gravity and rare jumps | MK3 somersault jumps | Keep the genre. Tekken's director lists realistic-gravity jumps among the reasons raw fighter capture needs rework ([Denfaminicogamer](https://news.denfaminicogamer.jp/kikakuthetower/241227u)) |
| Damage | Damage accumulates, and one clean shot can end a fight | 90-second rounds and health bars | Show damage building up on the body. Keep dramatic events rare |
| Heavy bodies | A fallen Mark V diver needs at least four people to raise him ([X-Ray Mag](https://xray-mag.com/content/heavy-metal-hardhat-diving-experience)) | A playable walk of 2.0 m/s | Sell the mass with anticipation, a settle after each step and a slow-looking get-up inside the same frames |
| Purity of style | One lineage, with real constraints | Fireballs, teleports, finishers | Sifu kept a split kick that is not Pak Mei because it was fun ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/shang-chi-vs-sifu-interview)) |

The principle behind every row is the same: **put realism in the joints and exaggeration in the clock**. Ishiguro builds his animation in three layers. Realism (physics, anatomy, kinesiology) is the base, acting sits on top, and *keren-mi* (showmanship) is the top layer. He argues that knowing the real mechanics makes the exaggeration convincing ([4Gamer](https://www.4gamer.net/games/991/G999104/20260728025/)). The New Virtua Fighter producer draws the same line between "reality" and "realism": copying a martial art directly would just make a game about that art ([AUTOMATON](https://automaton-media.com/articles/interviewsjp/new-virtua-fighter-20251124-366678/)). Sifu's consultant Benjamin Colussi puts it plainly: "if we only use realistic movements, it won't feel like a game anymore" ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/shang-chi-vs-sifu-interview)). Tekken's experience shows the risk from the other side. A professional boxer's captured body uppercut was technically sound but "too plain", and making it read nearly meant rebuilding the animation ([Denfaminicogamer](https://news.denfaminicogamer.jp/kikakuthetower/241227u)).

In Slopbat terms, the body mechanics should be true to life: weight, the order of the chain, balance, the striking surface and the direction of each reaction. The timing should stay "game": startup, recovery and hit-stop. The exaggeration should live in brief holds tied to contact, where it reads as impact rather than cartoon.

Responsiveness sets a hard edge on all of this. Steve Swink puts "real-time" response at under about 100 ms, or 6 frames ([Univ. Bayreuth lecture deck](https://medienwissenschaft.uni-bayreuth.de/wp-content/uploads/GxD-07-Interfaces.pdf), search summary). Weight therefore belongs in hit-stop and recovery, not in a slower startup. The cheapest check is the one the game-feel research suggests: capture play at 60 fps and count the frames from a button press to the first visible motion and the first sound. The project's existing 60 fps window capture can do this.

## Motion capture and physics belong where frame data stops binding

Every studio in the research treats mocap as raw material. Tekken's animation director calls it "a supplement". Every captured clip is hand-edited, many captures are rejected, some moves are entirely hand-keyed, and performers are told to move naturally rather than act, because acting spoils their form ([Denfaminicogamer](https://news.denfaminicogamer.jp/kikakuthetower/241227u)). Other studios cast real practitioners for authenticity. NetherRealm's animators hand-keyed from Eric Jacobus's footage before capturing him for eight MK11 characters ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/features/interviews/interview-the-youtube-star-who-became-kratos/)). Capcom captured a professional boxer for SF6's Ed and Kenny Omega for Alex ([EventHubs](https://www.eventhubs.com/news/2024/mar/04/capcom-ed-professional-boxer-sf6/); [Worthplaying](https://worthplaying.com/article/2026/1/21/news/148763-street-fighter-6-adding-street-fighter-iiis-alex-in-march-motion-capture-by-wrestling-superstar-kenny-omega-screens-trailer/)). For Virtua Fighter 2, staff who could perform kung-fu forms were assigned to motion production ([SEGA](https://vf20th.sega.jp/interview131211.html)). No studio publishes a numeric method for retiming captured moves to startup, active and recovery frames. It is in-house craft.

For Slopbat, the key-pose animator stays the authority. Mocap played back as clips for strikes would fight both it and the frame data. Even EA's motion-matched UFC 3 plays strikes "1:1 — there's no blending happening there" ([DualShockers](https://www.dualshockers.com/ufc-3-interview-marc-de-vellis/)). Licensed or commissioned capture earns its cost in three places. The first is **pose reference**: take the load, contact and follow-through extremes of a real performance into the pose files, then retime them by hand. The second is **direct playback where frame data binds loosely**: idles, walk cycles, intros, win poses, get-ups, KO falls and the victim's side of throws. The third is a **reaction vocabulary** for item 4. Motion matching suits footwork, and For Honor's Simon Clavet notes that it mostly needs "the positions and velocities of the feet" ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/programming/most-inspiring-game-animation-tech-talks-of-2016)). However, Slopbat's animator is its own solver mapped onto a 53-bone skeleton (`Anim/SlopMeshRig`), not Unreal's animation graph, so motion matching would be a large structural change worth deferring.

If a capture session happens, an inertial suit avoids optical markers being hidden during grappling, as Jacobus notes ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/features/interviews/interview-the-youtube-star-who-became-kratos/)). Tekken used an Xsens suit for quick, on-demand capture ([Denfaminicogamer](https://news.denfaminicogamer.jp/kikakuthetower/241227u)). Casting should follow the roster: a Thai-trained fighter for Ashka, a wrestler or judoka for the throws, and practitioners of bagua and Bujinkan for Mirela and Nix. The research did not evaluate any specific licensed mocap library, so the value of off-the-shelf packs is untested here.

Physics has to stay cosmetic until the outcome is fixed. Fight Night Round 4's physics knockouts gave "tremendous variety" but looked "completely unrealistic", according to UFC 2's creative director. UFC 1's canned knockouts then became repetitive, so UFC 2 returned to physics ([God is a Geek](https://www.godisageek.com/2015/11/ea-sports-ufc-2-interview), search summary). The safe hybrid plays an authored knockout onset for the first frames, so the read and the timing stay deterministic, and then hands off to a powered ragdoll whose strength fades while it follows an authored fall. Unreal's Physics Control plugin drives each child body relative to its parent with spring-dampers ([Epic API](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/API/Plugins/PhysicsControl)), and the older Physical Animation component scales motor strength from 0 to 1 ([Epic API](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/Engine/UPhysicalAnimationComponent)). Slopbat would need a physics asset per fighter, a hand-off from its solved pose, and a pelvis held on the fighting plane. The status of these plugins in UE 5.8 is unverified. Authored KO falls that include the fencing response are the better first step, with ragdoll added only if more variety is wanted.

### Ashka: square hips, a light lead leg and a kick that rises

Ashka's style has the most measured data behind it, so she can be the most exact fighter on the roster. The Thai stance is tall, with the hips square to the opponent, "not 'bladed' as in Western boxing". The feet sit just wider than the shoulders and the rear foot rests on its ball. The weight is "roughly even" and rocks between the feet ([MuayThai.com](https://muaythai.com/?p=627)). The popular rule of "weight on the back leg" is only half right: the rear leg is loaded on the ball of the foot, which frees the lead leg to check, teep or knee without a visible weight shift first. The guard puts the thumbs level with the eyebrows, palms facing each other and elbows below the hands, close to the body. The same source names palms-forward, elbows-raised guards as the common media error.

The idle should loop as a slow back-to-front rock rather than a boxer's bounce. A front-foot tap can be a separate defensive idle, and coaches name a faster "bounce" and a slower "plod" tempo ([fightrecord.co.uk](https://fightrecord.co.uk/?p=321)). Going forward, the lead foot steps first; going back, the rear foot does, so the legs never cross ([MuayThai.com](https://muaythai.com/?p=627)).

The roundhouse needs Ashka-specific keys, since her data overrides only two basics (`hp2` and `lk`). The standing foot rises onto its ball and the heel pivots outward. The hip drives through. The leg swings as a fairly straight, "passive" unit, like a baseball bat, while the kicking-side arm sweeps down and back and the other hand stays at the face ([Wikipedia: Roundhouse kick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roundhouse_kick); [Fairtex](https://fairtexstore.com/en-es/blogs/news/how-to-throw-a-thai-roundhouse-kick-breaking-down-the-technique)). The measurements back the look. Muay Thai kickers extend the knee more slowly than karate or taekwondo kickers (**706 vs ~945 deg/s**) and *rise* into the kick, with a peak vertical centre-of-mass velocity of **1.24 m/s** and **0.3–0.45 m** of vertical travel ([Gavagan & Sayers 2017](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0182645)). It should read as a rising swing, not a planted snap. How far the hip should turn over is disputed. One experienced contributor argues that top Thai fighters rotate subtly, so a moderate turnover is the safe choice ([8limbs.us forum](https://8limbsus.com/muay-thai-forum/topic/252-arms-position-when-kicking-what-works-best)).

Her elbow and spin chains should use the named shapes: *sok tad* (horizontal, thrown like a hook), *sok ngad* (the upward elbow, one of the fastest, which slips through the guard in the clinch) and *sok klap* (spinning, with the back briefly turned) ([Yokkao](https://uk.yokkao.com/blogs/muay-thai-news/muay-thai-elbow-strikes)). In the clinch, the rear foot comes up beside the lead foot, the hips stay close and the weight sits on the balls of both feet ([MuayThai.com](https://muaythai.com/?p=627)). The clinch-fighting style (*Muay Plam*) is built on posture control, off-balancing and sweeps ([Evolve MMA](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/understanding-the-muay-plam-style-in-muay-thai-and-the-art-of-clinching/)), which suggests her throws should be a clinch, a knee and an off-balancing dump.

Her shared crouch-block pose can hint at the check without changing any rule, by angling the lead shin outward. One account describes a Muay Chaiya instructor who checked by angling the shin without lifting the foot ([8limbs.us](https://8limbsus.com/?p=11865), search summary). For a real check, the shin is lifted near vertical with the knee turned out about 45°, meeting the kick near the top of the shin ([Evolve MMA](https://evolve-mma.com/blog/how-to-check-kicks-in-muay-thai)).

### Bram: an upright, anti-grappling guard and a stick that pivots

Under the London Prize Ring rules, a round ended only when a man went down, followed by 30 seconds' rest and 8 more to reach the scratch line unaided. There was no round limit. Holds and throws above the waist were legal, while butting, kicking, gouging and hitting a downed man were fouls ([Wikipedia: London Prize Ring Rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Prize_Ring_Rules)). The traditional stance "was actually designed to combat against the use of grappling as well as block punching" ([Wikipedia: Bare-knuckle boxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bare-knuckle_boxing)). Daniel Mendoza kept one arm across the body and the other around the head ([BoxingScene](https://www.boxingscene.com/what-fist-glove--5454), search summary). The claim that bare-knuckle guards held the elbows wider than 90° rests on a weak source ([Wikipedia: Classic Guards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_Guards_(boxing_style))).

The design's "forward-leaning boxer" is therefore a modern, gloved posture. The period look is upright with a more open chest: the lead fist forward at roughly chin-to-chest height, the rear forearm across the stomach, and the weight planted enough to resist being seized.

Without gloves, fighters favour body shots to protect their hands ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/sports-and-leisure/bare-knuckle-boxing)), though most of that evidence comes from modern bare-knuckle bouts. Mendoza blocked and struck with the same hand, and threw a backhanded "chopper", often straight after a block ([Wikipedia: Daniel Mendoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Mendoza)). That is a ready-made look for Stand Down, his 3-frame counter, keeping the same frames. Hooks are period-fair, since Tom Spring is credited with popularising the left hook and Samuel Elias the uppercut ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bare-knuckle_boxing)). Even so, Bram's head shots should be mostly straight blows and his hooks mostly to the body.

His forward throw is a natural fit for the chancery, a headlock held while punching with the free hand. His back throw fits the cross-buttock, with the hip in and the opponent levered over it. Both were legal ways to end a round, and Egan's account has Molineaux throwing Cribb with a cross-buttock in 1811 ([Fighters Only](https://fightersonly.com/article/ext/44561/Issue+022/1), search summary). A walk back to the scratch line makes a strong period beat at the start of a round.

The truncheon was "about one foot long". It struck by "bringing the arm down sharply while allowing the truncheon to pivot nearly freely forward and downward", so the tip moves much faster than the handle. Before the 1970s officers often struck the head with a full overhand swing ([Wikipedia: Baton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baton_(law_enforcement))). An 1841 Bedfordshire deposition records a constable striking an advancing man on the arm ([Bedfordshire Archives](https://bedsarchivescat.bedford.gov.uk/Details/archive/110344318), search summary). His baton moves should therefore be loose-wristed downward chops at the forearm, collarbone or head, with the free hand up to push and grab, and no twirling or tonfa handling. Ishiguro's weapon rule applies too: bring the weapon's mass toward the body's axis first, then accelerate with the spine and shoulder blade, because wide arcs do not read as heavy ([4Gamer](https://www.4gamer.net/games/991/G999104/20260728025/)).

### Gorm: sliding feet, a synchronised charge and the push-out

Sumo reads through a deep, wide squat with a straight back: knees bent, feet angled slightly out, and arms forward with the elbows tucked "like you are ready to grapple" ([Japan Society](https://www.japansociety.org.uk/usercontent/ea82a127db47401d4a8689723fdbb881/Sumo-Training-Moves.pdf)). In *suriashi*, the sliding walk, the feet "always remain connected with the ground", so Gorm should walk without bob or lifted steps. One stable visit describes heel and toe flicks, so a strict "heels never lift" rule is not supported ([Sumo Fan Magazine](https://sumofanmag.com/content/Issue_11/Heya_Peek3.htm), search summary). *Teppo*, the pole-pushing drill, advances the same-side hand and foot together, which is how his thrusts should look ([Japan Society](https://www.japansociety.org.uk/usercontent/ea82a127db47401d4a8689723fdbb881/Sumo-Training-Moves.pdf)). *Shiko*, raising a leg "as high as possible" and stamping it down, a ritual with Shinto roots in driving spirits from the ground ([Ian Burgess](https://www.ianburgess.me.uk/sumo/), search summary), suits Ground Slap and his intro.

The *tachiai*, the initial charge, starts from a squat on the balls of the feet, "torsos ramrod straight", fists on the clay, with the two wrestlers synchronising the start themselves. "The force at faceoff is proportional to the wrestlers' heft" ([Nippon.com](https://www.nippon.com/en/japan-topics/d00933/)). Rendering Charge's first frames should drop into that fists-down crouch and then explode forward. *Kachi-age* (a forearm smash) and *nekodamashi* (a clap in front of the face) are authentic variants ([Wikipedia: Tachi-ai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tachi-ai)). The commonest winning techniques are force-outs: yorikiri at 32.4%, plus *oshidashi* (push-out) and *tsukidashi* (thrust-out). *Tsuridashi* lifts the opponent and carries him out ([Wikipedia: Kimarite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimarite)), which suits Vat Press.

At 2.12 m Gorm is taller than Akebono, who stood about 203 cm, won with long-reach thrusts and was "top-heavy and susceptible to throws" ([Wikipedia: Akebono Tarō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akebono_Tar%C5%8D)). That suggests long-arm thrusting play, and a larger, slower stagger whenever he is knocked off balance.

Catch wrestling adds holds "anywhere you can". Pins were the classic win, and submission holds were mostly used to control the opponent and force the pin ([Wikipedia: Catch wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catch_wrestling)). The Snake Pit in Wigan teaches the double wristlock standing, in the clinch and on the ground ([SnakePit Wigan](https://snakepitwigan.com/?p=6142)). Gorm's grabs should therefore show a crank that controls the opponent into a slam, rather than a tap-out.

### Mirela: toe-in, toe-out and a body that never stops coiling

Baguazhang's defining practice is walking a circle in a low stance, facing its centre with the upper body twisted inward. The body coils and uncoils constantly, open palms pierce and push, and evasion aims to get behind the attacker ([Wikipedia: Baguazhang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baguazhang)). Three basic steps underlie "all the complex and amazing foot changes": toe-forward (*shun bu*), toe-in (*kou bu*) and toe-out (*bai bu*) ([Hsing-i.com](https://hsing-i.com/bagua-body), search summary). In a Ma Gui-style palm change the inside foot steps toe-in and settles firmly before the other foot steps toe-out ([Ma Gui Bagua](https://maguibagua.ca/mgbghome/eight-animals-or-models/2-dragon/2-3-dragon-following-palm-change/)).

Slopbat's simulation lives on a line, so the circle has to be suggested. The visual root can drift a few centimetres toward and away from the camera on a shallow arc as she walks, with the torso held twisted toward the opponent. Every turn-round on cross-over, and every Veil Step, should be a visible toe-in then toe-out pair. That pivot plus the spiral of the torso is the most recognisable bagua signature, more than any single strike. This adaptation is an inference, not sourced practice.

Lineage choice matters. Yin style emphasises "threading" palms and fast long-range striking, which suits a zoner. Cheng style emphasises pushing palms, close wrestling and joint locks ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baguazhang)). Hand shapes differ too: Yin's "ox-tongue" palm versus Cheng's "dragon claw" with the thumb spread wide ([Wikipedia: Cheng-style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheng-style_baguazhang)). The classics put the gaze out over the lead hand's thumb–index web, the "tiger's mouth" ([Shaolin.org](https://mail.shaolin.org/general-2/baguazhang/baguazhang01a.html)). The step also varies by lineage, between a gliding mud-step and a lifted step ([MartialTalk](https://www.martialtalk.com/threads/some-of-my-videos-on-youtube.120436), forum). Whichever is chosen should be used consistently.

The design's "tall floating" stance can coexist with bagua's low one: bend her knees under the gown so the hem and torso drop, and let the veil and her posture carry the height.

### Nix: an oblique, sunk stance and a chain that needs room

*Ichimonji no kamae* is the basic Bujinkan stance. The back leg keeps the body upright, the open hands protect or intercept, the shoulders are relaxed and the hips turn to hide the vital points. Its purpose is to pull back and dodge, and its precursor puts most of the weight on the back leg ([kogakure.de](https://kogakure.de/en/kamae/)). How much weight is disputed, with claims of 90% against the Jinenkan's 60/40 ([MartialTalk](https://www.martialtalk.com/threads/jinenkan-s-ichimonji.69541)). The design's "crouched ninja stance" should become an oblique body with a sunk rear leg and an upright spine. A hunched, round-backed crouch reads as the film ninja, an image *The Believer* traces largely to a 1983 film by a B-movie director ([The Believer](https://www.thebeliever.net/ninjas-i-have-known/)).

Evasion steps diagonally back and off-line, then re-enters. Strikes drive through the opponent's structure and stay connected rather than snapping back: "Once we're in, we like to stay there" ([Inside Ninjutsu](https://www.insideninjutsu.com/why-the-bujinkan-punch-is-different)). That gives Nix the distinct follow-through Ishiguro asks for. His recoveries should lean into the opponent while everyone else's retract.

The real kusarigama swings its weighted chain in a large overhead circle, whips it out to entangle a weapon or bind the limbs, then closes in with the sickle. Because one hit seldom stops an opponent, the chain must be reeled in for a follow-up. The specialist Yamada Shinryūkan was killed in a bamboo grove where he had no room to swing ([Wikipedia: Kusarigama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kusarigama)). Undertow, the chain trip, is therefore Nix's most authentic special. Sickle Throw is his least: it would read better as the weight thrown on the chain and then visibly reeled back during recovery, with the sickle kept for close cuts and hooks. The frames can stay as they are.

### Hollis: 86 kilograms of rig and a helmet that cannot turn

The Mark V rig weighs about 190 lb (86 kg) on the surface: a helmet and breastplate "a little over 55lbs", an 84-lb belt, 17.5-lb lead-soled boots each and a 30-lb dress ([Divers Institute of Technology](https://diversinstitute.edu/programs/suit-up/mark-v); [WHOI](https://images.whoi.edu/view-item?i=139884)). The helmet is fixed to the suit, so the diver looks out of a side port rather than turning his head. A fallen diver needs at least four people to raise him ([X-Ray Mag](https://xray-mag.com/content/heavy-metal-hardhat-diving-experience)). Under water he advances "in a stooping or crawling position" into current, and the air in the dress lightens his upper body. Tools hang on lanyards "slipped over the diver's right forearm", and he vents air by pressing the chin button inside the helmet ([US Navy Diving Manual 1943](https://archive.org/stream/DivingManual1943/DivingManual1943_djvu.txt)).

His animation should follow the rig. Steps are wide, flat-footed and short, with the torso forward over the hips and the arms held off the body by stiff canvas. Turns, including the turn-round on cross-over, rotate the whole suit as one block, with the neck locked. The lead-soled boots swing like pendulums, so kicks stay low and heavy. Get-ups roll to hands and knees and push up with a long settle, all inside the existing knockdown frames. A lanyard from the harpoon gun to the right forearm grounds an invented weapon: no source documents hard-hat divers using harpoons. Vent can read as a chin-valve purge of steam.

### The Slopbat: mass and telegraphs, with nothing real to borrow

None of the research covers a winged giant, so everything here is inference from general principles. Ishiguro's three cues for reading power transfer directly: mass ejection and reaction, instability as the centre of mass shifts, and stiffness at the instant of impact. So does his rule for heavy weapon arcs, which applies to claw swipes ([4Gamer](https://www.4gamer.net/games/991/G999104/20260728025/)). Telegraphs against a computer opponent can be more generous than in player-versus-player fights, because nobody has to fit them into real human reaction times. That claim comes only from a low-authority student thesis ([UAL](https://lucylueng.myblog.arts.ac.uk/2021/11/27/thesis05-reaserch-methodology/)). Sakurai's "boss stop", a zoom and a dramatic pause on the defeating blow, matches Slopbat's existing quarter-second freeze on the deciding hit ([EventHubs](https://eventhubs.com/news/2022/aug/24/masahiro-sakurai-youtube-channel-games), search summary). The wings should visibly load before Wing Gust, the same anticipation the Dive's shadow already provides.

## Conclusion

The most concrete finding is also the smallest. One ease curve (`snap`) and the static hold after it shape every strike in the game, and they run opposite to how real limbs move: the fist arrives at zero speed and stops instead of driving through the target. Replacing that curve, adding a drive-through key and pinning contact during the freeze would change the feel of every hit without moving a single box, and it goes straight at the "clunky, robotic" verdict on the 2.0.0 combat.

The research also shows that realism is cheapest in the frames the rules care least about. Idles, walks, recoveries, get-ups, the victim's side of a throw and the fall after a knockout are where style is recognised and where frame data does not constrain the animation. They are also exactly where licensed mocap fits. Startup, active frames and hit-stop should stay game-timed. The order that follows is real mechanics first, game timing second, brief exaggeration at contact third, and physics only once the result is decided.

Several things remain unmeasured: weight distribution and guard height in a fighting stance, the direction a head actually turns under each punch, how long a liver shot takes to fold someone, and anything about winged giants. For those, reference footage and the fighters' own practitioners should outrank any number in this report.
